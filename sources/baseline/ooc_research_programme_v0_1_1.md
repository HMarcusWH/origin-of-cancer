Executive verdict
The programme survives both an external research sanity check and an internal dumbassery check, but only after
narrowing several claims and hardening the research order. The central question is scientifically legitimate: driver
mutations, clonal expansion and even sizeable mutant fields do not by themselves define cancer; early malignant
transformation is shaped by tissue context, competition, stromal and immune state, developmental history and
selection. Recent work directly demonstrates both elimination of nascent lesions by neighbouring mutant tissue and
reciprocal niche construction that enables early lesions to persist. [W4-W8]

  Core thesis - provisional
   Cancer origin should be treated as a boundary-relative, partially observed transition problem: when does a
   declared non-malignant biological system enter an established malignant regime, through what ancestry-
   continuous causal route, and which route components are constitutive under admissible perturbation?


The strongest novelty is not "cancer is a regime transition", "cancer is evolutionary", or "the microenvironment
matters". All have substantial prior literatures. The candidate contribution is the explicit conjunction of declared
origin boundary, route closure, ancestry continuity, malignant-regime semantics, persistence, intervention-relative
constitution, partially observed multi-shadow inference, and evidence/certificate separation. [W1-W3, W10]

    Provisional thesis
    Cancer does not necessarily originate at the first oncogenic alteration. An Origin-of-Cancer claim is a boundary-

    a causally and genealogically supportable route. The strength of the claim depends separately on malignant
